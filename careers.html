01 / What it's like

Very few people, very close to the bench.

AstraDx is small on purpose. There is no layer between the person who designs the optical path and the person who runs the plate, because frequently they are the same person.

That has consequences worth being honest about. You will own things outside your job title. You will see your work in an instrument within weeks, not quarters. You will also occasionally be the one who has to figure out why the enclosure doesn't close. If you want a narrow, deep, well-scoped role inside a large organization, this is genuinely not it.

The constraint is the product

Anyone can build an accurate instrument with an unlimited budget. Our whole thesis is doing it at a cost that lets it be deployed where the burden actually is. Cost is a design input here, not a procurement afterthought.

Physical and computational, together

The optics team and the ML team are not sequential. What the sensor can resolve determines what the model can learn, and what the model needs determines what the sensor has to resolve. Expect to work across that boundary.

Regulated for real

We are building toward FDA-level performance on a wide range of pathogens. That means the rigor is not optional — traceability, validation, and honest error bars are part of the daily work rather than a phase at the end.

Funded to do the hard version

CARB-X and the NIH back this program specifically because it is the difficult, non-obvious approach. We have runway to do the science properly rather than ship a demo.

02 / Open roles

Where we're hiring.

Click any role to expand. If you're close but not exact, write to us anyway — with a team this size, we shape roles around people more often than the reverse.

Micro-Optical Engineer Optics · Beverly, MA · Full-time

You own the light path — from illumination through to sensor — for an instrument that has to resolve bacterial-scale behaviour reliably, repeatedly, and cheaply enough to manufacture at volume.

What you'd do

  • Design and prototype micro-optical assemblies: illumination, imaging, and sample interface.
  • Model and characterise resolution, depth of field, aberration, and stray light against what the imaging pipeline actually needs.
  • Drive tolerance analysis and design-for-manufacture on optical components and their mounts.
  • Build and run optical benches, and turn bench results into design decisions quickly.

What we're looking for

  • Degree in optical engineering, physics, or equivalent hands-on experience.
  • Fluency in optical design software (Zemax, Code V, or similar) plus real bench instincts.
  • Experience with microscopy, imaging systems, or instrumentation for biological samples.
  • Comfort designing to a cost target, not just a performance target.
Medical Microbiologist Microbiology · Beverly, MA · Full-time

You are the ground truth. Every claim the instrument makes about an organism and its susceptibility has to survive comparison against reference methodology — and you own that comparison.

What you'd do

  • Design and execute studies across clinically relevant bloodstream pathogens, including resistant isolates.
  • Run reference AST methods (broth microdilution, disk diffusion) as the comparator for instrument performance.
  • Maintain organism banks, culture workflows, and the aseptic discipline the whole program depends on.
  • Translate CLSI/EUCAST breakpoints and FDA expectations into concrete study design.

What we're looking for

  • Advanced degree in microbiology or a closely related field, or equivalent clinical lab experience.
  • Hands-on phenotypic AST experience and familiarity with CLSI standards.
  • BSL-2 competence and genuine care about contamination control.
  • Willingness to work directly alongside engineers who will ask you naive questions.
AI / ML Imaging Scientist Machine learning · Beverly, MA · Full-time

The signal is in the images long before it is visible to a person. You build the pipeline that pulls it out — and, just as importantly, you establish how much we're allowed to trust it.

What you'd do

  • Develop image processing and deep learning models for pathogen detection, identification, and growth-response measurement.
  • Work upstream with the optics team on what the sensor should capture in the first place.
  • Build honest validation: held-out isolates, failure analysis, calibration, and uncertainty — not just headline accuracy.
  • Ship models that run on constrained on-instrument compute, not a datacenter.

What we're looking for

  • Strong background in computer vision and modern deep learning, with real Python engineering ability.
  • Experience with scientific or microscopy imaging, and comfort with small, expensive, imbalanced datasets.
  • Statistical rigor — you should be the person most suspicious of your own results.
  • Bonus: experience in a regulated or clinical setting.
Clinical Biochemist Assay development · Beverly, MA · Full-time

Whole blood is a hostile sample. You own the chemistry that gets from a raw neonatal draw to something the optics can read, without losing the organism along the way.

What you'd do

  • Develop and optimise sample preparation and assay chemistry directly from whole blood.
  • Work within the constraints of small-volume neonatal samples.
  • Characterise reagent stability, shelf life, and lot-to-lot variability for a manufacturable consumable.
  • Partner with engineering on how the chemistry becomes a cartridge rather than a protocol.

What we're looking for

  • Advanced degree in biochemistry, clinical chemistry, or related.
  • Assay development experience for diagnostic products, ideally from complex matrices.
  • Design of experiments as a habit rather than a technique you've heard of.
  • Interest in the whole path from bench chemistry to a shippable consumable.

03 / How to apply

Email us. That's the whole process.

There is no applicant tracking system to disappear into. Send a note to lab@astradx.com with the role in the subject line, a CV, and a few sentences on what you'd want to work on here.

Links to real work — papers, repositories, instruments you've built, assays you've validated — are worth more to us than a cover letter. If we think there's a fit, you'll hear from a person, not a template.
